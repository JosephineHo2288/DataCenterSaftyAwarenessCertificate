##### 1.2 Why Elevated Work in Data Centers Is High-Risk
Working at heights across hyperscale data centers introduces complex hazards distinct from generic construction sites. Elevated work zones encompass white space overhead containment trays, high-voltage busway gantries, mechanical equipment mezzanines, cooling tower fan decks, and central chiller plant rooftops.

Even a fall from seemingly modest elevations—such as a 4-foot step stool or raised staging curb—can produce severe traumatic head injury, musculoskeletal fractures, or fatalities.

* **Primary High-Elevation Zones:** Rooftop cooling towers, backup generator radiator banks, overhead cable trays, ceiling-mounted busways, and air-handling unit (AHU) filter plenums.
* **Secondary Environmental Multipliers:** Congested overhead valve systems, high-temperature glycol piping, arc flash boundaries near live switchgear, high wind shears across parapets, and slick condenser condensate.
* **General Protection Threshold:** Fall protection is required for all tasks at elevations of **4 feet (1.2 meters)** or higher (with construction-specific contractor thresholds sometimes set at 6 feet / 1.8 meters).

##### 1.3 The Physics of Falls: Gravitational Potential Energy & Impact Forces
In O-Level Physics, an elevated body possesses Gravitational Potential Energy ($E_p$):

$$E_p = mgh$$

$$\text{Kinetic Energy at Impact: } E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = mgh$$

$$\text{Impact Force } (F) = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \frac{m \Delta v}{\Delta t}$$
